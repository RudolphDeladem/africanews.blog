Accra's Kinectiks Robotics is designing one autonomous machine for many farm jobs

The startup says African farms do not need more labour, they need the right machines. Its answer is a single electric robot meant to scout, spray, mow and carry. It is still in development, and the company says so.

By the africanews.blog tech desk · 16 November 2026 · 5 min read

Open the Kinectiks Robotics website and the first thing you notice is what is missing. There is no robot. The pictures are of people: a line of farmers weeding at sunset, a man with a sprayer on his back in a maize field, two farmers filling woven baskets with tomatoes. Each is captioned the same way. Spraying, by hand. Breaking ground, by hand. Harvesting, by hand.

That is the company's argument in three pictures. Kinectiks, based in Accra, says it is developing a modular robotic platform that will do repetitive agricultural and outdoor work on its own. The machine is not finished, and the site marks it plainly as "in development".

The problem it describes

Kinectiks starts from a simple observation: across Africa, millions of farmers still do by hand the repetitive jobs that machines took over elsewhere decades ago. They mow, spray, monitor and harvest, the company says, in extreme heat, on difficult terrain, with ageing equipment, rising costs and fewer people in rural areas willing to do the work.

Its conclusion is the line the site sets in the largest type: "Africa doesn't need more labour. It needs the right machines." The company is careful about where it places the blame. "The problem isn't a lack of effort," it writes. "It's a lack of appropriate automation."

The word "appropriate" matters. Large tractors and combine harvesters are built for big, flat, single-crop fields. Many African farms are small, irregular and broken into scattered plots, which is one reason imported machinery often does not fit them.

One platform, four kinds of work

Rather than a separate machine for each job, Kinectiks describes a single autonomous platform that takes on different tasks. It groups them under four headings.

OperationTasks the company lists
GrowCrop monitoring, field scouting, weed detection, data collection
ProtectPrecision spraying, pest management, field inspection, crop protection
MaintainAutonomous mowing, vegetation control, grounds maintenance, path and row clearing
MoveHarvest assistance, material transport, farm logistics, flexible payloads

The list reaches beyond the farm gate. Mowing, vegetation control and grounds maintenance are jobs on estates, campuses and roadsides too, which fits the company's description of its work as "agricultural and outdoor tasks".

How it is meant to work

Kinectiks explains the machine as a loop of three steps.

Sense

LiDAR, cameras, sensors and wheel encoders read the field. LiDAR measures distance with laser pulses, and encoders count wheel rotation so the robot knows how far it has travelled.

Think

Software the company describes as AI perception and decision making interprets what the sensors see and plans the work.

Act

The machine moves and carries out the task without a driver.

This is the standard structure of an autonomous robot. The hard part is making it work reliably outdoors, in dust, on uneven ground and among crops that look different every week.

Designed around African conditions

The company lists four design requirements for the platform.

  • Built for Africa. It must cope with uneven terrain, dust, heat and fragmented fields.
  • Reliable. Rugged hardware designed for real conditions.
  • Electric. The company says this means lower running costs and less harm to the environment.
  • Scalable. One platform with many applications.

These are goals, not test results. Kinectiks has not published specifications such as battery life, payload, speed or the size of field one machine could cover in a day.

Who is behind it

Kinectiks calls itself "African-born" and "built in Accra". It describes itself as a robotics and automation company that designs, engineers and deploys intelligent robotic systems to remove repetitive physical labour, raise productivity and improve quality of life.

Its ambitions are not limited to farming. The stated vision is "to be Africa's leading robotics company, building intelligent machines that transform how the world lives and works". The mission is to deliver "smart, accessible and powerful robotic solutions that put the world in motion, one machine at a time". Agriculture is where it has chosen to begin.

The website does not yet name its founders or team. Its menu lists pages for About, Products, Team, News, Solutions, Research and Development, Gallery and Careers, but at the time of writing only the home page was published.

What is not known yet

A good deal. Kinectiks has not shown a prototype, published a price, named a launch date or announced pilot farms, customers or investors. The images on its site show hand labour and farmland, not a machine. The company appears to have made that choice on purpose: it presents the problem and its plan, and labels the platform as unfinished.

That candour is worth noting, but it also means every claim about the robot is still a statement of intent. Field robotics is expensive and slow to get right, and the cost of the finished machine will decide whether a smallholder, a cooperative or only a commercial farm can use it.

Why it matters

Kinectiks is not alone in seeing the opening. Another Accra startup, 3Farmate, founded in 2021, is also developing autonomous robots that carry out several farming tasks, according to We Are Tech Africa. Two companies in one city working on the same problem suggests the idea is being taken seriously.

What Kinectiks adds is a clear statement of the design brief: small and fragmented fields, heat, dust, low running costs, and one machine that earns its keep across the whole season instead of sitting idle after a single job. Whether it can build that machine is the question its next update will need to answer.
