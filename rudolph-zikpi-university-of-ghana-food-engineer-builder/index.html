From award-winning food packaging to code and robots: meet Rudolph Zikpi

A University of Ghana engineering student whose team took two international packaging awards in Milan. He also writes software, from an electronics simulator to campus apps.

By the africanews.blog tech desk · 16 November 2026 · 4 min read

Disclosure: Rudolph Zikpi runs africanews.blog. This profile is based on public reporting and his public work.

A win in Milan

In October 2025, three University of Ghana students returned from Italy with two awards, Graphic Online reported. Rudolph Zikpi and Mary Eyram Chukwu of the Department of Food Process Engineering, and Richmond Oteng Kumah of the Department of Food Science, won the Best in Food Category Award and a Silver Award in Food Packaging at the 2025 International Student Packaging Design Competition in Milan. Their design stood out among 237 entries.

To get to Milan, the team first won a national competition hosted by Royal Crown Packaging Ltd, beating teams from KNUST, Accra Technical University, Academic City University College and Ashesi University.

What EcoFlex does

Their project, EcoFlex, takes on one of the stubborn problems of farming in Ghana: keeping fresh produce from spoiling between the farm and the buyer. It has two parts.

  • EcoFlexCool keeps produce fresh using evaporative cooling, the effect that cools you when sweat dries, so it needs no electricity.
  • The EcoFlex Basket is a biodegradable container that stacks and folds flat. It has drainage, adjustable compartments and tracking features.

Cooling without electricity matters in a country where many farms and markets lack reliable power, and where a large share of fruit and vegetables is lost before it is sold.

The students presented their awards to the University of Ghana's Vice-Chancellor, Professor Nana Aba Appiah Amfo. "I'm excited because you have shown that University of Ghana students can do it," she said, according to Graphic Online. The team was mentored by faculty including Professor Elsie Effah Kaufmann, Dean of the School of Engineering Sciences.

Studying on a scholarship

Zikpi studies on a University of Ghana scholarship. In a testimonial published by the university's financial aid office, he wrote: "As a student with limited financial resources, pursuing higher education has always been a challenge." The scholarship, he said, lets him concentrate on his studies without the weight of financial worry.

The builder

Outside the lab, Zikpi writes software. His public GitHub account, RudolphDeladem, holds a run of projects, many aimed at Ghanaian users:

  • Simscuit, a browser-based electronics lab, which we covered earlier
  • the website of Kinectiks Robotics, an Accra startup designing farm robots, also covered here
  • LegonRush, described as "the campus lifestyle reimagined", and a Legon campus ride project
  • a point-of-sale system, a carbon monitor and a landing page for Micaiah AI

The thread running through it is practical: tools for students, farmers and small businesses, built for conditions in Ghana.

Why it matters

Ghana's tech story is often told through founders who studied computer science. Zikpi's path is a reminder that useful technology also comes from other disciplines. A food engineer who understands post-harvest loss, and can also write the software and build the website, is the kind of person the country's farm-tech and education-tech sectors need.
