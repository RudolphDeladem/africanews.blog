Ghana's young robot builders are world champions. Next stop: Puerto Rico

In May, a team of children from Accra won Ghana's first Robofest Junior World Championship. In December, six Ghanaian teams take on about 500 others at the World Robot Olympiad.

By the africanews.blog tech desk · 16 November 2026 · 5 min read

Ghana has won Robofest world titles before, but at senior level, with secondary school students. In May 2026 it won one with children.

Team Apex from Mikrobot Academy became champions of the Junior Division at the 2026 Robofest World Championships in Michigan, United States, beating 26 teams from 19 countries, the Ghana News Agency reported. It was the first time Ghana had won the Robofest Junior World Championship title.

The team

The five members of Team Apex were Faiz Sornyiina Abdul-Wahab, Tiana Lilian Naa-Chardey Taylor, Israel Yeboah Awuah, Inaaya Khan and Aalia Mehreen. Their rivals came from countries including China, Taiwan, Canada, Mexico, the United States, Hong Kong, Greece, Macau and South Korea.

Dr Michael Wilson, a research scientist at the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research's Institute for Scientific and Technological Information (CSIR-INSTI), called the win "a testament to how a dedicated group of engineers in Ghana is grooming the next generation".

What Mikrobot Academy is

Mikrobot Academy is a private robotics and STEM club, set up in 2014, that aims to keep young people interested in science and engineering careers. Its learners are aged between eight and 15. Its teams come from branches in Accra, Kumasi, Tarkwa and Tema.

The Robofest effort was coordinated by the Ghana Robotics Academy Foundation (GRAF) and supported by Brownstone Construction, the Ghana AI Research Network, CSIR-INSTI and the Responsible Artificial Intelligence Lab, according to the Ghana News Agency.

Other Ghanaian results

Right to Dream Academy finished second in the Senior Division. Its team also earned a university scholarship package worth US$64,000 from Lawrence Technological University, which hosted the event, Yen.com.gh reported.

Bosomtwe Girls STEM Senior High School, a school opened about five years ago under the Ministry of Education's STEM schools programme, came fourth in the Time Trial Bottle Sumo category. In that event, robots must find obstacles and push them off a raised table against the clock.

The new title adds to Ghana's earlier senior-level Robofest world titles, won by Methodist Girls' Senior High School in 2019 and Prempeh College in 2020.

Next: the World Robot Olympiad

In July, six Ghanaian teams won places at the 2026 World Robot Olympiad (WRO) finals in Puerto Rico, which the Ghana News Agency says bring together about 500 teams from nearly 80 countries in December. They qualified at a national competition organised by GRAF at the University of Ghana, Legon, where 52 teams competed across 11 challenges.

In RoboMission, teams build and program a robot to complete a set task on a game board. Future Innovators asks teams to design a robot that solves a real problem, and Future Engineers centres on self-driving vehicles.

Mikrobot Academy has three of the six places, one for its Accra branch and two for Kumasi. The University of Ghana's ARTEMIS team carries the country's hopes at university level.

Why it matters

Robotics competitions are a small stage, but they test the skills that matter in the economy Ghana says it is trying to build: programming, electronics, mechanical design and teamwork under pressure. The organisers behind Ghana's run of results argue that robotics teaches problem solving that is useful whatever career a child ends up in.

What stands out is the spread. Ghana's competitors no longer come only from a few elite secondary schools. They include children from a private club, a five-year-old girls' STEM school, a football academy and a public university.
